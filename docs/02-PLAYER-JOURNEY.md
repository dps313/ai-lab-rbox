# AI Lab Tycoon — Player Journey

**Status:** Draft 0.1 — intended experience, not a technical implementation plan

## Phase 1: The garage — first discovery

**Fantasy:** *I have almost nothing, but I can build something useful.*

- Spawn with **$100**, a personal garage, desk, case, monitor, keyboard, and mouse — but no functioning PC.
- Clear first objective: get a CPU, GPU, and RAM.
- Travel to a **physical shared computer store**. Browse dependable new hardware and limited bargain-bin used stock among other players.
- Buy parts, return, install them through a handful of tactile interactions, power on the PC with satisfying feedback.
- The guaranteed new starter combination should be affordable from starting funds; the existing prototype reportedly prices it at $95.

**Moment of delight:** The first computer boots.

## Phase 2: The first AI product

**Fantasy:** *I made something people will pay for.*

Preferred (not finalized) interaction:

1. On the powered PC, start a small, recognizable project, potentially a two-category classifier (e.g. animal images).
2. Choose relevant starting data or a project setup.
3. Make a **small number of understandable strategy choices**. Prefer a *Game Dev Tycoon*-style process over manually engineering neural networks.
4. Choose **discrete, logarithmically scaling training investment**, initially perhaps Quick / Standard / Intensive.
5. Watch lively processing blocks, flowing data and training feedback.
6. See a release/result reveal with a clear explanation of why it performed as it did.
7. Name and publish the model, beginning a comprehensible in-game revenue stream.

**Moment of delight:** The player has created their first published AI model and sees earnings.

**Unresolved:** The exact screens, pacing, choices, evaluation attributes, and whether there are intermediate development decisions. Do **not** implement this flow from this outline alone.

## Phase 3: Improve it

**Fantasy:** *I know how to do better next time.*

- Browse hardware again; consider a GPU upgrade, a good used deal, improved data, or a different training plan.
- Make a successor, such as `PetVision 2.0`, and compare it to the existing release.
- Learn that compute expands feasible scale and training throughput, while choices and data matter to the result.
- Experience diminishing returns from brute-forcing one model; see reasons to diversify.

**Critical product test:** Does the player voluntarily create a second version or another model?

## Phase 4: Hobbyist — repeated core loop

**Fantasy:** *My setup and collection are becoming impressive.*

- Collect increasingly rare or unusual components; inspect store restocks.
- Decorate or improve visible garage/workstation setup.
- Develop multiple niche products and experiment with strategies.
- Choose between better hardware, improving an existing lineage, launching something new, or saving capital.
- Develop meaningful short- and long-term objectives without overwhelming menus.

## Phase 5: Startup — first automation [Future]

**Fantasy:** *I built a company, so I don't have to do everything myself.*

- Hire specialists, contract out labeling or tech work, automate formerly manual tasks, add workstations and simple server infrastructure.
- Responsibilities shift toward deciding projects, resource allocation, procurement, recruiting and delegation.
- Employee quality and expertise matter beyond flat production multipliers.

## Phase 6: AI corporation [Future]

**Fantasy:** *I run a major player in the AI industry.*

- Build and customize offices, server rooms, data centers.
- Source bulk hardware, manage power/efficiency, rent compute or serve customer demand.
- Compete for high-risk, high-reward contracts; pursue technical, commercial, or infrastructure strategies.
- Later flavor opportunities: marketing decisions, press conferences, reputation, employee poaching and market events.

These future stages are direction, **not authorized features**.

## Experience principles to validate

- First objective and shopping interaction understandable without instruction walls.
- Manual actions tactile but not tedious, with automation eventually replacing them.
- Idle training gives meaningful alternative activities rather than mandatory waiting.
- Players understand at least one reason an improved model differs from its predecessor.
- Every session leaves an obvious next goal.
- Earlier ideas such as first model within ~5 minutes are **test hypotheses**, not fixed requirements.
